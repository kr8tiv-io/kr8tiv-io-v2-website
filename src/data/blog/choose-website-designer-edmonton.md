# How to choose a website designer in Edmonton

Hire the person who can make it obvious what you do, where you work, and how to contact you. Pretty layouts are easy to buy. A site that helps an Edmonton or Alberta customer decide to call is a narrower skill, and you can test for it before you sign.

This is a buyer’s guide, not a pitch disguised as a checklist. KR8TIV designs websites in Edmonton for businesses across Alberta and Canada. Use the same questions if you are talking to us or to someone else. The goal is a quote you can compare, a process you understand, and a site you will still own next year.

If you are still deciding whether a rebuild is even the right spend, start with the guide on [when a custom website pays off for an Alberta business](/blog/website-that-earns-its-keep-alberta/). This article assumes you are ready to hire, and need a way to choose well.

## What should a first conversation actually uncover?

The first call should be about the job the website has to do, not about fonts. A useful designer will want to know what you sell, who buys it, where you actually work, and what a good enquiry looks like.

For a fictional Sherwood Park electrician, that might mean residential service calls inside a defined travel area, plus the occasional commercial fit-out. Those are different buyers. If the designer never asks who should hire you, they are decorating a homepage.

Ask these questions out loud:

* Who will write the words, and who approves them?
* Who supplies photographs, and what happens if the current ones are weak?
* How do enquiries arrive, and who answers them?
* What do you keep after launch: domain, hosting login, source files, and the right to change the site?
* What is included in search setup, and what is explicitly not a ranking promise?

Listen for specific answers. “We handle all of that” is not a scope. “You keep the domain and hosting accounts, we send a handoff document, and search setup means titles, descriptions, a sitemap and working internal links” is a scope.

## How do you compare website quotes without comparing unlike jobs?

Put every quote on the same short schedule. The lowest number often describes a thinner job, not a better deal.

| Scope item | What to record |
|---|---|
| Pages and purpose | The actual pages, and the decision each one helps a buyer make |
| Words and images | Included, supplied by you, or quoted as extra |
| Enquiry path | Form, phone, booking, and a tested confirmation |
| Search setup | Titles, descriptions, sitemap, redirects if you already have a site |
| Ownership | Accounts, source files, and who can update the site later |
| Ongoing costs | Hosting, maintenance, and anything that renews |
| Timeline and revisions | What you must provide, and how feedback is handled |

If one proposal is a five-page site with copy, photography direction and a working form, and another is “a new homepage” with stock images you must find yourself, those are not competitors. They are different purchases.

Ask where Alberta and Canadian details will show up. If prices are in CAD, they should be labelled. If you serve Edmonton, St. Albert and Spruce Grove, the site should say so without pretending you have a staffed office in every community you drive through. If you work across the province, say that plainly too.

A small test helps. Before you choose, ask each designer how they would explain your offer on a phone-sized homepage. You do not need a free custom mockup. You need to hear whether they can name the service in a sentence a stranger would understand.

## Which promises should make you walk away?

Walk away from a guaranteed first-place ranking in Edmonton. Nobody can honestly sell that, and the people who try often spend the project talking about tricks instead of making the business easier to hire.

Be equally wary of:

* A portfolio you cannot open on a phone, or work that never names a real service.
* A process that disappears after kickoff, with no named person accountable for drafts.
* A contract that leaves the designer holding your domain or your only copy of the files.
* A quote that is only a vibe, with no mention of forms, redirects, or what happens to your old URLs.

You do not need to be technical to catch this. If you cannot explain, in one sentence, what you will own on launch day, the agreement is not finished.

Founder-led work is not automatically better, but you should know who is actually designing and building. Ten layers of account management can turn a simple Alberta trades site into a game of telephone. KR8TIV’s [process](/process/) is deliberately short for that reason. Other studios can be equally clear. The test is whether they will say who does the work.

## What does a useful brief look like?

You do not need a 20-page deck. You need enough fact that two designers would quote the same job.

Write this down before you request prices:

> We want a website that helps [buyer] in [service area] decide to [enquire / book / call] for [specific service]. The current problem is [unclear offer / broken form / outdated site / no site]. We can provide [photos, logo, testimonials], and we still need [copy, new photography, or a clearer offer]. Budget range: [your range]. Launch constraint: [date or none].

For an illustrative Edmonton roofer, the buyer might be a homeowner with a leaking valley, the area might be Edmonton and Sherwood Park, and the next step might be a photo-based quote request. That brief is already more useful than “we need a modern site.”

Bring three examples of sites you like, and one you do not, with a note about why. “I like how easy it is to find the emergency number” is a brief. “Make it award-winning” is a mood, and moods are expensive to interpret.

Look at [selected work](/work/) the same way you would look at anyone else’s portfolio. Ignore the theatrics for a minute. Can you tell what each business does? Could you contact them? That is the bar.

## A scorecard you can use on the call

Give each designer a pass, a question, or a no on the same points. Do not award points to the nicest moodboard.

* They can repeat your service and service area back to you without dressing it up.
* The quote lists pages, content responsibilities, and the enquiry path.
* Ownership and ongoing costs are written down.
* They will not promise a Google ranking, and they can explain what search setup does include.
* You know who will do the work and how feedback happens.
* The timeline depends on real inputs from you, not a magic number of days.

Two “questions” are fine. A “no” on ownership or on a ranking guarantee is usually the end of the conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a website designer in Edmonton cost?

It depends on the job, not the city name. KR8TIV’s published floors are $1,200 CAD for a full website and $600 CAD for a focused landing page, with final scope agreed in writing. Treat any quote that skips copy, photos, forms, ownership and ongoing costs as incomplete, not cheap.

### Should I hire a local Edmonton designer or someone remote in Alberta?

Hire the person who will do the work, understand your buyer, and stay reachable. A local studio can help with in-person reviews and Alberta context. A strong remote designer can also serve you well if the process, ownership and communication are clear.

### Can a website designer guarantee I will rank first in Edmonton?

No honest designer can guarantee a Google position or an AI recommendation. They can set up clear pages, titles, internal links and a usable enquiry path. Ranking depends on the market, the content, and how buyers search, not on a promised placement.

### What should I send before asking for a website quote?

Your main service, the area you actually serve, your current site if you have one, common customer questions, approved photos or a note that you need them, a budget range, and the problem you want solved. That is enough to get a comparable quote.

## Choose the partner who can explain the work

A good Edmonton website designer will make the decision feel smaller, not more mysterious. You should leave the call knowing what is included, what you must provide, and what the site is for.

Bring Matt the brief, even if it is still rough. [Start a website conversation](/start/) and use the same questions on that call that you would use anywhere else.
