# How much does branding cost in Edmonton in 2026?

Branding in Edmonton costs anywhere from a few hundred dollars to well past $30,000. That range is useless on its own, so here is the useful version. A logo on its own usually runs $400 to $3,000. A full identity from a local studio that publishes its prices runs about $2,400 to $6,400. Strategy-led work and rebrands start around $7,000 and climb from there.

Those are three different purchases that happen to share one word. Most of the confusion about branding prices comes from comparing a $300 logo with a $5,000 identity and wondering why one studio is "so expensive." They are not selling the same thing.

This post lays out what Edmonton studios and Canadian pricing guides actually publish, what you should walk away with at each level, the costs that never make it onto the quote, and a simple way to choose a budget. I checked every price on 22 November 2026. KR8TIV's own prices are near the end, clearly labelled, so you can skip past them or hold me to them.

## What counts as "branding"?

When someone says branding, they usually mean one or more of these:

* **A logo.** The mark and the name, drawn properly, in the versions you need.
* **A visual identity.** The logo plus colours, typography, image style and the rules that hold them together.
* **Brand guidelines.** A written guide that shows anyone, including your next designer, how to use the system without guessing.
* **Messaging.** What you say about yourself, in what voice: the one-line description, the tagline if you need one, the way you answer the phone.
* **Naming.** Finding a name that is available, pronounceable and not already owned by someone with a lawyer.
* **Applications.** The identity designed onto the real things: website, business cards, invoices, social profiles, signs, vehicles, workwear, packaging.

A logo is one item on that list. A full identity is most of it. If you want the longer argument about which one you need first, read [logo or brand](/blog/logo-vs-brand-small-business/). This post is about what each one costs.

Two things jump out. First, the studios cluster. Most published Edmonton identity packages land between about $2,400 and $6,400, which is a much tighter band than the national guides suggest. A quick tax note, since this is Alberta. There is no provincial sales tax here, so the only tax on design work is the 5 per cent GST, and only from businesses registered to collect it. Some studios show prices with "+ GST" and some do not. When you compare quotes, compare the totals.

## What you should actually get for the money

This is where cheap and expensive branding really differ. Not in how the logo looks on a laptop screen in the first meeting, but in what lands in your inbox at the end.

A proper handoff includes:

* **Vector master files.** SVG, AI, EPS or PDF. Vectors scale from a favicon to a billboard without going fuzzy. A PNG alone is not a logo file. It is a picture of a logo.
* **Every version you will need.** Horizontal, stacked, icon only, and name only, each in full colour, one colour, black and reversed for dark backgrounds.
* **A small-size version.** A favicon and a social avatar that still read at the size of a thumbnail.
* **Colour values for every job.** HEX and RGB for screens, CMYK for print, and a Pantone match if you will ever print merch or signage.
* **Fonts, with the licence sorted.** The names of the typefaces, where they came from and who holds the licence. Open-licence fonts such as Google Fonts cost nothing to license. Commercial fonts can carry fees for desktop seats, web traffic or apps.
* **A written guide.** Short is fine. It should show clear space, minimum sizes, colour use and a few right and wrong examples.
* **Ownership.** The final files and the rights to them should belong to you once you have paid. Get that in writing.

At the identity level, add the applications that matter for an Alberta business: the website header, the Google Business Profile image, business cards, invoices and quotes, email signature, social templates, and anything physical that has to be made later, such as signs, truck doors and shirts.

## What changes the price

Two studios can quote the same "logo and brand guide" and land $3,000 apart. Usually, one of these explains it:

**Strategy and research.** Interviews, competitor reviews and positioning happen before anyone draws anything. A founder who already knows exactly who the customer is needs less of this than a company with three partners who describe the business three different ways.

**Concepts and revision rounds.** Three developed directions with reasoning behind each are more work than ten quick sketches. Bloc includes one round of changes and bills extras. Others include more. Neither is wrong, but you should know which one you are buying.

**Naming.** Naming is its own project, with its own price. RISE publishes $1,000 to $2,400 for standalone naming. A shortlist of names is not legal clearance, so budget for a trademark search as well.

**How many applications are designed.** "Applications" on a proposal can mean "we will show your logo on a mockup of a mug" or "we will design your vehicle wrap, sign face and packaging to print." The second one costs much more, and it should.

**Languages and locations.** English and French versions, or a brand that has to cover several locations or sub-brands, add work.

**Rebrand baggage.** A new business starts clean. A rebrand has to plan around old signs, wrapped trucks, printed stock and the customers who already know you. Unalike estimates the rebrand premium at 20 to 40 per cent over branding from scratch.

## The costs that never make it onto the quote

The design fee is rarely the whole bill. These are the lines I see people forget:

* **Trademark filing.** The Canadian Intellectual Property Office charges [$491.06 for an online application in the first class](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/trademarks/fees-trademarks) of goods or services in 2026, plus $149.04 for each additional class. Those rise to $499.41 and $151.57 on 1 January 2027. A lawyer or agent's fees are on top, and are worth it if the name matters.
* **Business name registration and licences.** Those are separate from branding, and [starting a business in Edmonton](/blog/starting-a-business-edmonton/) walks through them with real fees.
* **Printing and fabrication.** Designing a sign is not making a sign. Cards, wraps, signs and shirts are quoted by the printer or fabricator. In Edmonton, most signs also need a City permit, covered in [Edmonton sign permits](/blog/edmonton-sign-permits/).
* **Font licences.** See above. No licence fee for open-licence fonts, an annual or one-time fee for many commercial ones.
* **Photography.** An identity can set the photo style. Someone still has to take the photos.
* **The website.** A new identity on an old website looks like a new suit with muddy boots. Budget for the site update, or read [what a website costs in Edmonton](/blog/website-cost-edmonton/).
* **Extra rounds and changes later.** Find out the hourly rate for work outside the scope before you need it.
* **Rollout.** Replacing old signs, stock and uniforms is where rebrands quietly get expensive. Plan it in stages if you have to.

## Red flags in a branding quote

Some warning signs are easy to spot once you know them:

* **Raster-only delivery.** If the final files are only PNG and JPG, you will be paying someone to redraw your logo the first time a sign shop asks for a vector.
* **"Unlimited revisions."** It sounds generous. It usually means there is no process, and you will be the process.
* **Fifty concepts in 48 hours.** Volume is not thinking. Ask how the concepts were chosen.
* **No written scope.** If the deliverables are not listed, you cannot tell what you bought.
* **No mention of ownership.** You should own the final files and the rights to use them, in writing.
* **A logo that falls apart small or in one colour.** Ask to see it at favicon size, embroidered size and in black only. If the designer has not tested that, you are the test.
* **A template sold as custom.** Marketplace logos are often resold. If the mark has to be trademarked, it has to be original.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Bring these to any designer, including me:

1. What exactly is delivered at the end? Can I see a sample handoff folder?
2. How many concepts, and how many rounds of revisions, are included?
3. What happens, and what does it cost, if I need more?
4. Is any research or strategy included, or am I bringing the brief?
5. Which applications are designed, not just shown on a mockup?
6. Who owns the final files and the rights, and when does that transfer?
7. Which fonts are used, and who pays for the licence?
8. Is GST included in the price?
9. How long will it take, and what do you need from me to keep it on schedule?

A good studio will be happy you asked. A nervous one will change the subject.

## DIY or hire someone?

There is an honest case for doing it yourself. If you are testing an idea, have no customers yet, or need something usable this week, a clean template and a good name will carry you a while. Unalike makes the same point: pre-revenue businesses can reasonably spend $0 to $200 and move on.

It is time to hire someone when the logo is about to go on something expensive or permanent. Signs, vehicle wraps, packaging runs and embroidered workwear are where a DIY logo gets costly, because each one locks the mark in and each one has to be redone if the mark changes. If you are about to pay for a wrap, paying first for a mark that will survive the wrap is the cheaper order of operations.

## A simple way to choose a budget

Count the places your brand will appear in the next 12 months. Then pick the tier that matches.

* **One to three places,** such as a website, a social profile and a business card: a well-made logo with proper files is enough. Plan for roughly $500 to $1,500.
* **Four to ten places,** such as adding a sign, a truck, invoices and shirts: you need a kit or an identity, so the colours and type stay consistent when different suppliers produce them. Plan for roughly $1,200 to $6,500.
* **More than ten, or several locations, or a crowded market:** you need strategy and a full identity. Plan for $7,000 and up.

Then ask one more question. What would it cost to redo all of that if the mark has to change in two years? Every sign, wrap and box printed with a weak logo is a bill waiting to be paid twice. That cost is usually what makes the middle tier the sensible one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a logo cost in Edmonton?

A logo on its own usually costs between about $400 and $3,000 CAD in 2026. Freelancers sit at the low end, around $400 to $1,500, and small studios at roughly $1,500 to $4,500 when discovery and proper file delivery are included. Contest sites such as 99designs list logo packages from US$249 to US$1,050.

### How much does a full brand identity cost in Edmonton?

Edmonton studios that publish package prices list full identities from about $2,400 to $6,400 CAD, before GST where it applies. Identity work led by research and positioning usually starts around $7,000 and can pass $15,000. Rebrands for larger companies through bigger agencies can run from $35,000 into six figures.

### How long does a branding project take?

Most small business identity projects take three to eight weeks. Published Edmonton timelines include three to six weeks at BL3SSED, four to six weeks for Hotspot Creative's SPARK package and four to eight weeks at Lula Creative. Naming, research or a slow feedback loop adds time.

### What files should I get at the end of a branding project?

At minimum, vector master files such as SVG, AI or PDF, every version of the logo including one-colour and reversed, a small icon for favicons and social avatars, colour values for screen and print, the names and licences of your fonts, and a written guide that shows how it all fits together. You should own the final files outright.
