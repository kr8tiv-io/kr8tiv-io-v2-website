# Logo or brand? What an Alberta small business actually needs first

Most small businesses start by asking for a logo. Good instinct. You need something on the truck, the invoice, the storefront and the profile picture, and you need it soon.

A logo is one piece of something bigger, though. Knowing the difference helps you spend your budget on the right thing at the right time, whether that is a single mark today or a full identity later.

Here is how I explain it to founders and small business owners around Edmonton on a first call.

## What a logo is

A logo is a symbol or a wordmark. It is the shorthand for your business, the thing people see in a split second.

A good logo is simple, works at small sizes, works in one colour, and still reads on a dark background or a dusty work truck on the Whitemud in February. That last part matters more than most people expect. A logo that only looks right on a white screen will cause problems the first time you print a shirt.

If you are just getting started, a clean, well-built logo with the right file formats is a perfectly sensible first step.

## What a brand is

A brand is everything people recognise as you, even when your logo is not on it.

It is your colours, your type, the way your photos look, the way you write a quote, the way your website moves, and the way you answer the phone. A logo tells people your name. A brand tells them what to expect from you.

Here is the quick test I use. Cover the logo on your website, your truck or your last flyer. Could someone still tell it is yours?

If yes, you have a brand. If not, that is the opportunity. There is room to make everything you put out look and sound like one business instead of a pile of separate pieces.

## What a brand identity usually includes

"Brand identity" is the working toolkit that makes a brand repeatable. On a [KR8TIV branding project](/services/branding/) it means a logo, a system and a story. In plain terms:

- **The logo, in every version you will need.** Full, stacked, icon-only, light and dark, and the file formats for print, web and social.
- **A colour palette.** A few colours with clear jobs: the main one, the accent and the neutrals.
- **Typography.** One or two typefaces, and rules for headlines versus body text.
- **A voice.** How you sound in writing: short or detailed, formal or friendly, and the words you always or never use.
- **A style guide.** One place that shows how it all fits together, so whoever makes your next flyer, sign or post gets it right without guessing.

The style guide is the part people skip, and it is the part that keeps a brand consistent when you are not the one doing the design.

## When a logo is enough, for now

A logo on its own makes sense when:

- **You are testing an idea** and are not sure the business will look the same in a year.
- **You need something usable this week** for a sign, a uniform or a launch.
- **Your budget is small.** That is fine. KR8TIV takes small one-off pieces too, and starting small is a real way to start.

Just make sure the logo is built well enough to grow into a full identity later, so you are not starting over.

## When it is time for a full brand identity

It is usually time when:

- **You are launching or relaunching.** A new name, a new offer or a new market is the moment to set the first impression on purpose.
- **More than one person makes your materials.** If your printer, your nephew and a website builder are all making things, a system keeps them consistent.
- **You compete on trust.** Trades, local services and founders selling something new need people to believe them quickly. Consistency does a lot of that work.
- **Your materials don't match.** If the website, the truck and the business card each look like a different company, a brand ties them together.

The same system then carries into the physical world. A [storefront sign](/blog/storefront-sign-design-edmonton/) or [packaging](/blog/packaging-design-that-stands-out-canada/) is much easier to get right when the brand rules already exist.
