# Template or custom website? How Edmonton small businesses can decide

Every small business owner hits this question eventually. You need a website. You can pick a template this afternoon, or you can have one designed around your business. Both are real options, and both make sense for different people at different moments.

Here is how I think it through as someone who builds websites in Edmonton for a living and still tells some people to use a template.

## What a template is good at

A template is a ready-made layout. You swap in your logo, your words and your photos, and you are online.

That is genuinely useful. If you are testing an idea, need a page up before a market weekend at the Old Strathcona Farmers' Market, or just want your hours and phone number findable, a template is a smart move. It is fast, it is affordable, and the big website builders keep them updated.

So if that is where you are, start there. There is no shame in a starter site.

## What a template can't do for you

A template is built to fit as many businesses as possible. That is its strength, and it is also its limit.

The layout, the rhythm of the page and the way it moves were all decided before anyone knew your business existed. You can change the colours and the fonts. The structure was designed for everyone, so it can't be designed for you.

Try this. Cover the logo on your homepage. Could a visitor still tell whose website it is?

If yes, you have a brand. If not, that is the opportunity. There is room for your site to say something only you can say.

## When a custom website is worth it

A custom website starts from your business instead of from a layout. It makes sense when:

- **You compete on trust.** Trades, local services and founders selling something new all need people to believe them quickly. A site that clearly looks and sounds like you does a lot of that work.
- **You are launching or relaunching.** New offer, new name, new market. That is the moment to set the first impression on purpose.
- **Your story is the product.** If people buy from you because of who you are and how you work, the site should show that, not just list services.
- **You have outgrown the starter site.** If you spend more time working around the template than working in it, that is a sign.

## Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

AI is part of how I work. It speeds up first drafts and the repetitive parts of a build, and that is a big reason custom work costs less than it used to.

AI does not decide what your business should feel like. It can produce a page in seconds. It can't tell you which page is yours. That part is taste, and taste comes from understanding your business, your customers and what makes you different.

So the robots do the typing. A person makes the calls.

## How to decide

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I just need to be findable, or do I need to be remembered?** Findable is a template's job. Remembered is a custom site's job.
2. **Is this a test or a foundation?** Test with a template. Build the foundation custom.
3. **If I covered my logo, would anyone know it is me?** If the answer is no and that matters to your business, it is time.

There is no wrong answer here, only the right answer for where your business is today. Whichever way you go, keep your business name, phone number and service area identical on the site and on your Google Business Profile. That consistency helps people and search engines trust what they find.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a website template good enough for a small business in Edmonton?

Often, yes. If you are testing an idea, need your hours and phone number findable this week, or have a tight budget, a well-kept template is a smart start. A template is not a ranking penalty. Clear content, accurate business details and a usable phone experience matter more than how the site was built.

### When is a custom website worth it?

When you compete on trust, are launching or relaunching, sell something people buy because of who you are, or spend more time fighting the template than using it.
