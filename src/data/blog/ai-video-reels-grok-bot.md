# How we make AI video reels with Grok Bot

We make a short AI reel by giving Grok Bot the pipeline and keeping the taste with a person. The bot pulls the beats, prompts Grok Imagine, writes the compositor, and checks the frames, the kicks and the loudness. I approve the joke, the stills and the post, and nothing goes up until that yes.

A short reel is a 30 second argument. The setup has about 2.5 seconds to earn the title, the picture has to work with the sound off, and the model will not know which part is the joke. It will happily animate a grey website, a dinner plate and a monster. You still have to decide which one is funny.

The method below was locked on two of our own cuts, finished 26 and 27 November 2026, rejects included. The 26 November reel is the house standard. The 27 November reel had not been posted when this note was written.

## Key takeaways

| Point | What to remember |

## What is Grok Bot doing on the reel?

Grok Bot is not a caption button. xAI's guide, checked 27 November 2026, calls it an AI teammate with a computer. It works in connected apps and on a cloud computer, so it keeps going after the laptop is shut. It can use plugins and custom MCP servers, and it can hand coding to a Cursor cloud agent.

On our reels we give it six jobs.

1. Pull the beats out of the raw brief, and keep the lines that are a claim, a joke or a feeling.
2. Open a primary source for any number that will go on screen, and drop the number if the source cannot be opened.
3. Prompt Grok Imagine with the references, the action and a ban on text.
4. Write and run the compositor in Python, Pillow, numpy and ffmpeg: type, wordmark, beat grid, speed ramps, end card.
5. Check frame strips, loudness and kicks.
6. Log the script, the licence, the rejects and the decisions the same day.

xAI's use-case page tells you to keep sending, publishing, purchasing and production changes behind an approval. A bot that can post is an accident with your login.

## What does Grok Imagine actually make?

Three jobs, and a fourth that is not its job.

**Stills.** Prompt plus reference images, vertical 9:16. Lock the character, the expression, the prop and a dark area for type.

**Image to video.** The still is the first frame. xAI's docs, checked 27 November 2026, say image-to-video on `grok-imagine-video-1.5` is native 1080p, and a clip can run from 1 second up to 15 seconds. We generate 6 second takes. On the 26 November reel those files measured 1088 by 1920, 24 frames a second, 145 frames.

**First and last frame.** Pin both stills and the model fills the move. That is how a character grows without a cut. xAI treats that pin as reference-to-video, and reference mode tops out at 720p. Our first morph came back at 480p. We asked again at 720p and scaled the file up. Written down as a compromise, not as a win.

**Not its job.** Type, the wordmark, a price, a phone number and the spelling of a name go on in code, from the real logo file, after the picture is approved.

## How do we keep the same character in every shot?

Pass a clean, type-free reference into every later still. "A blue monster" will invent a cousin. The 26 November reel used a prior frame plus a hero sheet, and named "the same large fluffy blue monster with purple highlights and curved brown horns" and "the same small brass vintage robot with glowing yellow eyes" every time. The 27 November monster is teal, with a giant cyan-violet brain, plus that same robot. Do not mix the two monsters in one film.

A still prompt, shortened, with the rude title left out:

> A polished 3D film still, vertical 9:16. The same large fluffy blue monster with purple highlights and curved brown horns from the reference images, gleeful, ripping a blank grey page into scraps. The same small brass robot with glowing yellow eyes, both arms up. Cinematic dark background, heavy vignette, characters low in the frame. No text, no logos, no letters anywhere.

Characters stay low so the top third is dark enough for type. Screens stay wordless: grey rectangles for a template, magenta, orange and cyan for a custom site. Name the prop. A prompt that said knife switch once put a real knife in the robot's hand. The retake asked for the wooden handle of an old knife switch, on the wall, no blade and no weapon. Another still painted a car maker's emblem on the lab walls. The retake asked for plain walls. Check the still before you spend the clip.

## How does a still become a clip?

The video prompt is shorter, and stricter about speed.

> Real-time normal speed, no slow motion. The monster tears the page in fast cartoon timing. The robot throws both arms up. One gentle push-in. No text appears.

Review a strip of about nine frames. Reject surprise letters, a warped face, an extra limb, or slow motion. On a title shot add "Camera completely locked, no zoom, no push-in." The 27 November open pushed in and parked the brain under the hook. The reshoot stayed locked, and the compositor zoom was cut to 6 percent. A crash zoom, when we want one, is ours, and it lands on a kick.

## How do we cut motion so the reel does not feel like one trick?

Dark ground, heavy vignette, hard cuts, gold and cyan, the official wordmark. The motion changes every shot. The menu: crash zoom, a one-hit glitch, kinetic type that scales from about 3 times size down to 1 in 0.13 seconds, a tear that reveals the next picture rather than black, a whip on the type layer only (whipping the whole frame showed black edges), split screen, a zoom from inside the tablet out to the dinner, and a little parallax.

Speed ramps go fast to normal only. Never slow motion. One ramp on the shred plays at 1.6 times and eases to normal. Titles sit on detected kicks under about 150 Hz, not on a beat tracker that was a quarter beat off. On the 32 second reel an earlier music start, 11.40 seconds into the source, was that quarter beat late. The start moved to 11.14 seconds so the drop kick (source 13.72 seconds) hits the title at 2.58. In the final mix the kicks landed at 2.58, 3.22, 3.87 and 4.50 seconds, against slams at 2.58, 3.23, 3.88 and 4.53. Within a frame. The 45 second reel's drums are syncopated, so the title hits are hard-coded at 3.01, 3.95, 4.59 and 5.06 seconds.

Wordmark up top, mono kickers, setups in white `#ececec`, punchlines in italic gold `#c1a65e`. Nothing important in the bottom, where the platform buttons sit. A big head, like the 27 November brain, pushes copy to a lower third. Lines do not touch. The wordmark is the official file from `kr8tiv-logo-clean.svg`, never a redraw and never a heavier stand-in.

The 16:9 of the 32 second reel is its own layout: picture in a feathered panel on the right, type on the left, not a pillarbox. The 45 second reel still has no native 16:9. That gap is written down, not hidden.

The 32 second reel encodes as H.264 High, slow preset, CRF 19, AAC 256 kbps at 48 kHz, so playback can start before the download finishes. Each shape, the 9:16 and the 16:9, took about 4 minutes. The 45 second master is also H.264 High, with AAC at 256 kbps and 48 kHz. The log for that file records those settings and does not record a CRF, so the 32 second CRF stays with the 32 second reel.

## What about music, loudness and voices?

Licensed tracks only. Both reels use Mixkit hip-hop by Arulo: "Young Trizzy 02" and "Thunder". The licence index and the Stock Music Free License modal were opened 27 November 2026. The modal allows the music in commercial and non-commercial projects, including social video posts, online ads, YouTube, podcasts and educational use. It does not allow CDs or DVDs, TV or radio, video games, a music-only remix, claiming the track, or registering it with a rights service. The 26 November build note also records that attribution was not required on the modal that day. The modal opened on 27 November does not restate that line, so the note in the build folder is what we keep with the file, next to the title, the artist, the URL, the download day and a file hash.

Pick the track before the timing. The house target is about 80 to 100 beats per minute, a quiet intro, a drop for the title, and a break for the card. "Young Trizzy 02" starts at 11.14 seconds of the source. "Thunder", about 95.7 beats per minute, starts at 17.21 seconds, and its drop hits the title at 3.00 seconds. Fade the last 1.3 seconds on both. No separate sound effects.

> If you cannot name the licence, you have a takedown waiting for a quiet Tuesday.

LUFS is how loud the whole file feels. True peak is the spike, and a file over 0 clips when encoded. These marketing reels aim at about -14 LUFS, with the limiter set for a true peak of -1.5 dBTP. The 32 second reel landed at -14.0 LUFS and -1.1 dBTP, which clears "under -1" and does not hit the -1.5 target. Say the result, not the wish. The 45 second reel's Mixkit file peaked above 0 dBTP, and one loudness pass left the audio at -0.4 dBTP. A two-stage limiter brought that 45.00 second master to -14.8 LUFS and -2.1 dBTP.

These two reels do not speak. When a film does, use the rules from the Evolve Bigfoot shorts: one speaker per clip, the listener's mouth closed, the same voice sentence every time, generate about 6 seconds from a clean still and trim, speech inside the first half second, and watch the mouth. If a track is under the line, duck it. The Ogopogo log of 25 October 2026 records "Feel Good Funk Beat" by JorisVermeer under the Pixabay Content License, commercial use, no attribution, -16.0 LUFS and -1.3 dBTP. Beaver Tail has dialogue and a river ambience bed, no music, with the loudness target at -16 LUFS and a true peak of -1.5 dBTP.

A character kit is the folder of approved stills, the wardrobe, the voice sentence and the prop that must not change shape.

## What gets checked before anything posts?

Proof, fix, repeat. Then a contact sheet of the finals and a full-size frame at every cut. Type off the face. Lines apart. Kicks within a frame of the slams. No generated text. The official wordmark. Loudness measured on the finished file. The 16:9 on its own, when a 16:9 exists. Write down a dark whip edge, an upscaled morph, or a pointer gone for a few frames. A hidden flaw that is written down is a choice. A hidden flaw that is not written down is a surprise on a phone.

Then someone who did not just defend the cut reads it against the approved script. A self-check has passed a cut that the second look failed. Nothing posts without the owner's yes. On our reels that is me. On a client reel it is the client.

I will not invent an hour count. Measured: 32.00 seconds and 45.00 seconds, 6 second clips, six stills and six clips on the 26 November reel, about 4 minutes to encode each shape of that reel, and a second morph pass at 720p after 480p.

### Mistakes worth avoiding

| Mistake | What we do instead |
|---|---|
| Text inside the generated frame | Composite type and the real wordmark in code |
| Slow motion, or a raging character | Fast-to-normal ramps, and an arc of feelings |
| The same move on every shot | A different entry every shot |
| Trusting the beat grid | Detect the real kicks and lock the slams to them |
| A push-in on a shot that carries type | Lock the camera. Keep zooms in the compositor |
| Skipping the still check | Approve the still, then spend the clip |
| Unnamed music | A licence note with title, artist, URL, date and hash |
| Trusting one loudness pass | Aim near -14 LUFS on these reels, near -16 with dialogue, and measure the file |
| Posting because the render finished | A second review, then the owner's yes |
