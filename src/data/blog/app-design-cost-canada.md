# How much does it cost to design and build an app in Canada in 2026?

A custom app in Canada usually costs somewhere between $20,000 and $250,000 to build, and more for complex platforms. That is from budgets Canadian studios publish themselves. A clickable prototype costs far less, and the design work on its own is a fraction of the build.

That range is too wide to plan with, so this post breaks it down: what studios publish, what design costs as a separate step, what Apple and Google charge, and how to cut a first version down to something you can afford. I checked every price on 19 November 2026.

One thing to be upfront about. KR8TIV designs apps. We do the research, user flows, screens and prototypes. We do not write the code. Development is done by your own developers or a build partner agreed in the scope. So I have no reason to inflate the development numbers below.

The ranges overlap, which tells you something. "MVP" means different things at different shops. A five-screen app with one platform and a simple back end is a very different job from a two-platform app with payments, user roles and an admin dashboard.

## What drives the price

Roughly in order of impact:

**Number of platforms.** iPhone only, Android only, both, or a web app. Cross-platform tools can reduce the cost of supporting both.

**Accounts and roles.** An app where everyone sees the same thing is simpler than one with customers, staff and admins who each see something different.

**Payments.** Taking money means a payment provider, receipts, refunds and the app store rules about in-app purchases.

**The back end.** Where the data lives, the admin tools to manage it, and the integrations with your existing systems (booking, inventory, accounting).

**Offline use.** Apps for field crews that must work without signal and sync later take more engineering. Glasshead builds specifically for this.

**Security, privacy and accessibility.** Health, financial or children's data raise the bar. So does bilingual English and French.

**Maintenance.** Phones and operating systems update every year. An app nobody maintains starts breaking. Codazz publishes $2,000 to $8,000 a month for ongoing maintenance on its builds.

## What does app design cost on its own?

Design is the stage before code. It answers: who is this for, what do they need to do, and what does each screen look like? The usual deliverables are:

* **Research and flows.** Who the users are, what jobs they come to do, and the steps for each one.
* **Information architecture.** What goes where, and what each screen is called.
* **Wireframes.** Rough layouts, fast to change.
* **Interface design.** The finished look: type, colour, components, every state of every screen.
* **A clickable prototype.** Something you can put in a real person's hand and watch them use.
* **Handoff.** Organised design files, components and notes for the developers.

## What Apple and Google charge

The stores are a small cost compared with the build, but they are ongoing:

* **Apple.** The [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/) is 99 USD per membership year, listed in local currency when you enrol. Under the [App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), developers with less than 1 million USD in yearly proceeds pay a reduced 15 per cent commission on paid apps and in-app purchases.
* **Google Play.** A [one-time US$25 registration fee](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en). Google says that of the developers who pay a [service fee](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en), 99 per cent are eligible for a fee of 15 per cent or less.

If your app sells physical goods or real-world services, like booking a haircut, different store payment rules can apply. Check the current terms for your case.

## Do you need an app at all?

Ask this before anything else. Many small businesses that ask me about an app need one of these instead:

* **A fast, well-built mobile website.** It works on every phone, needs no download, and can be found on Google.
* **A web app.** For logged-in tools, bookings or dashboards, a web app can do most of what a native app does, without the stores.
* **An existing platform.** Booking, loyalty and ordering platforms exist for most trades and shops.

A native app makes sense when people will use it often, need phone features such as the camera, location or notifications, or need it to work offline. A [website](/blog/website-cost-edmonton/) is usually the better first step for a business that customers visit once or twice a year.

## How to keep version one affordable

1. **Write down the one job.** The single thing a user must be able to do in version one.
2. **Cut every screen that does not serve that job.**
3. **Pick one platform** if your users mostly use one.
4. **Use off-the-shelf services** for login, payments and email instead of custom-building them.
5. **Design and test before you build.** Put the prototype in front of five real users.
6. **Budget for year two.** Updates, fixes and the features you cut.
