# How Edmonton and Alberta businesses can show up in Google AI and ChatGPT answers

You improve your chances by making the business easy to name, easy to locate, and easy to quote accurately. You cannot buy a guaranteed mention in Google’s AI answers, ChatGPT, or any other assistant. Anyone who sells that is selling a wish.

People in Edmonton already ask these tools for a roofer, a web designer, or a shop that can restore a driveway. The answers are only as good as the public information those systems can find and trust. If your website is vague, your listings disagree, and your best proof lives in an unsearchable photo dump, you are hard to recommend. If you are specific, consistent and useful, you at least give the models something true to work with.

This is not a second version of our guide to [writing a business blog that earns enquiries](/blog/blog-that-ranks-and-brings-leads/). That article covers how to choose article topics and how Google describes its AI search features. This one is about the business itself: the service pages, the listings, the reviews and the facts a stranger should be able to repeat.

## What do these assistants actually draw from?

Different tools have different systems, and those systems change. The honest baseline is still public, checkable information: your website, your listings, your reviews, and other pages that describe the same business.

Google’s [generative AI search guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says its AI search experiences rely on core Search ranking and quality systems. It also says there is no special schema required for generative AI search, and that Google Search does not use an llms.txt file for visibility or rankings. Read that guide rather than a thread about secret tags.

For other assistants, the same humility applies. A page that is useful to a person in Sherwood Park is more likely to be useful to a system that is trying to summarise the market. A page stuffed with “Edmonton Alberta Canada SEO AI ChatGPT” is not a strategy. It is a sign you are writing for a rumour.

Technical hygiene still matters. If the important sentences only exist inside an image, a locked script, or a PDF nobody can open on a phone, you have hidden the very facts an assistant might need. A [website built with crawlable, readable pages](/services/web-design-edmonton/) is the foundation. It is not a citation certificate.

## How do you make the business easy to identify?

Write as if a careful stranger has to describe you in two sentences. They should be able to find:

* The real business name, used consistently.
* The services you actually sell, in ordinary language.
* The area you actually serve, and the limits that affect a quote.
* A way to contact you that still works.
* Evidence: work samples, a process, reviews, or other proof you have the right to show.

A fictional Leduc furnace company that never names the service on the homepage, lists “Greater Edmonton and beyond” without explaining travel, and buries the phone number in an image is asking both people and models to guess. A page that says “furnace repair and replacement in Leduc, Nisku and south Edmonton, with emergency call-outs when we can take them” is doing the job.

Keep the same facts in every public place. The website, the Google Business Profile, and any other listing should agree on the name, the phone number and the coverage. Assistants are not impressed by a slightly different name on each platform. They, and your customers, are more likely to get the story wrong.

Be careful with claims. If you cannot support “the best in Alberta” or “same-day everywhere,” do not publish it. A model that repeats an unearned superlative is not a win. It is your copy coming back as an embarrassment.

## What should you build instead of a gimmick?

Build service pages that answer one hiring question each, and keep them updated. If residential and commercial work are truly different, they may need different pages. If you work across Alberta, explain how travel and scheduling actually work. Do not manufacture a near-empty page for every town on the highway.

Reviews and other third-party mentions help because they are evidence outside your own marketing. They will not help if they are fake, and they should not be the only place a fact exists. Put the important information on your site first.

A short, factual about or process section can help too: who does the work, where you are based, and what a customer should expect. Edmonton and Alberta context belongs here when it changes the work, not as a repeated slogan.

If you also publish articles, let them do a different job from the service pages. The article helps someone think through a decision. The service page says you do that job and how to enquire. Link them. Do not clone them.

## What should you refuse to buy?

Refuse a guaranteed mention in ChatGPT, Gemini, or a Google AI answer. Refuse a special “AI file” sold as a ranking switch. Refuse a rewrite of every page that only adds assistant-related vocabulary.

You can ask a designer or an SEO person to inspect titles, service clarity, listings and crawl issues. You can measure what Search Console shows, including any AI-related reporting Google provides for your property. You cannot honestly treat a single assistant reply, on one day, as a strategy.

A useful review question is: “If Google disappeared tomorrow, would this page still help a customer in Edmonton hire us?” If the answer is no, it is not AI optimization. It is decoration.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get my Edmonton business into Google AI and ChatGPT answers?

Make the business easy to identify and easy to check. Use a clear website, accurate service pages, consistent name and location details, and evidence a stranger can trust. There is no special file or schema that guarantees a citation, and no studio can honestly sell a placement in an assistant’s answer.

### Is AI search optimization different from ordinary SEO?

The fundamentals are the same. Google says its AI search experiences rely on core Search systems. Useful pages, clear facts and a site that can be crawled still come first. What changes is the need to be quotable, with specific services, real coverage, and claims you can stand behind.

### Should I create a separate page for every Alberta city I serve?

Only when you have genuinely different information to offer. Swapping a city name into the same thin page does not make you more useful to a person or to a model summarising your site. Say where you work, clearly, and write pages around distinct services or questions.
