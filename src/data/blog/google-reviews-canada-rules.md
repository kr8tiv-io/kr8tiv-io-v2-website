# How to get more Google reviews in Canada without breaking the rules

The legal way to get more Google reviews in Canada is simple: ask every customer, the same way, soon after the job, with a direct link and nothing in return. No discounts, no draws, no asking only the happy ones, no reviews from staff. If you ask by email or text, follow Canada's anti-spam law.

Most of the trouble I see does not come from bad intent. It comes from a plan someone read about online, usually written for the United States, that breaks Google's rules or Canadian law. This guide covers three rulebooks: Google's own policy, the Competition Act, and CASL. I checked each source on 19 November 2026.

This is practical information, not legal advice. If your business sends a lot of email or text marketing, talk to a lawyer about CASL.

## What does Google allow?

Google wants you to ask. Its [guide to getting reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) suggests sharing a review link or a QR code with customers, and reminds owners that customers need a Google account to leave one.

What it does not allow is spelled out in its [Maps user contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en). Businesses must not:

* **Offer incentives**, such as payment, discounts, or free goods or services, for posting a review, or for changing or removing a negative one.
* **Discourage negative reviews or selectively ask for positive ones.** This is review gating. A survey that sends four- and five-star answers to Google and one- to three-star answers to a private form is exactly this.
* **Require or pressure customers to review while they are on the premises.** A tablet at the counter with staff watching is the usual example.
* **Ask for specific content.** "Could you mention our basement renovations in Sherwood Park?" is out.
* **Post reviews with a conflict of interest.** Google lists current or former employees, contractors, and family members.

The policy also bans setting staff a target number of reviews to collect.

## What happens if you break the rules?

Google removes reviews it believes break the policy. It can also [restrict a Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en), on top of removing the reviews. Google's examples include blocking new reviews for a set period, unpublishing existing reviews for a set period, and showing customers a warning that fake reviews were removed. Google emails the owner before it applies a restriction, and the owner can appeal.

That warning banner is worse than having fewer reviews. Anyone comparing two plumbers on Maps will notice it.

## What Canadian law says about fake and staff reviews

Google's rules are a contract. The Competition Act is the law. In January 2024, the [Competition Bureau warned businesses](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html) that reviews posted by employees without disclosing their connection can make the business liable. Its advice:

* Train employees to disclose their connection when they review the company or a competitor.
* If disclosure is not possible in the review, they should not post it. Employees should not leave star ratings where the disclosure is separated from the rating.
* Run a compliance program and monitor for misleading reviews.

The same release says the deceptive marketing provisions apply to anyone who promotes a product or business, and that the penalties can be significant. Buying reviews, writing reviews for yourself, or getting friends to review a business they never used all fall into the same territory.

## Where CASL comes in

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, or CASL, covers commercial electronic messages: emails, texts and direct messages. The [Act defines one](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-1.html) as a message where it would be reasonable to conclude that one of its purposes is to encourage participation in a commercial activity.

Is a review request a commercial electronic message? The Act does not say so directly, and I could not find an official ruling on that exact question. A review request plainly helps you win more customers, so the safe approach is to treat it as one. That means three things.

**1. Have consent.** Under [section 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-6.html), you need express or implied consent. The federal [guide to CASL consent](https://fightspam-combattrelepourriel.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) explains that an existing business relationship, such as a purchase, can give implied consent. Under [section 10](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-10.html), that lasts two years from the purchase, or six months from an inquiry. A customer you finished a job for last week is fine. A list of everyone who ever called you since 2019 is not.

**2. Identify yourself.** Every message must name your business, give contact details that stay valid for at least 60 days, and include an unsubscribe option.

**3. Honour unsubscribes fast.** [Section 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-11.html) gives you 10 business days to act on one.

The penalties are not small. [Section 20](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-20.html) sets the maximum administrative penalty at $1 million per violation for an individual and $10 million for a business. Nobody is fining a bakery $10 million for one review text. But a review tool that blasts your whole contact list with no unsubscribe link is an avoidable risk.

## A review request process that works

Here is the process I set up for clients. It is boring on purpose.

**Ask everyone, at the same point.** For a trade, that is the day the job is signed off. For a shop, it is on the receipt or the bag. For a service business, it is the follow-up email after the appointment. Same message, every customer, good day or bad.

**Use the direct link.** In your Business Profile, find the option to ask for reviews and copy the short link. Put it in the email, the text, and as a QR code on a small card or the invoice.

**Make it one tap.** Do not send people to your website first, or ask them to search for you.

**Reply to every review.** Google says replies are [usually checked within about 10 minutes](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en), but can take up to 30 days. Thank good reviews by mentioning the job. Answer bad ones once, calmly, with a way to reach you offline. Google notifies the reviewer when you reply, and they can still change their review after reading it.

**Track it monthly.** Number of jobs, number of requests, number of reviews. If requests are going out and reviews are not coming in, the message or the timing is wrong.

## Scripts you can copy

These are written to be honest, short, and safe under the rules above. Change the details to your business.

**Text after a job:**

> Hi Jordan, it is Sam from [business name]. Thanks for having us out today. If you have a minute, an honest review on Google helps other people in Edmonton find us: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out.

**Email after an appointment:**

> Subject: How did we do?
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> Thanks for coming in on Tuesday. If you are willing, we would value an honest Google review. It takes about a minute: [link]
>
> Whatever you think, we read every one.
>
> [Business name], [street address], Edmonton. [Phone number].
> Unsubscribe: [link]

**Counter card:**

> Tell people what you thought. Scan to review us on Google.

Notice what is missing. No "five stars", no "if you were happy", no discount, no contest entry.

## What about reviews you think are fake?

If a review breaks Google's policy, for example it is from a competitor, it is off-topic, or the person was never a customer, you can report it from your Business Profile. Google decides. Do not argue in public, and do not threaten the reviewer. Ask yourself first whether it might be a real customer you do not remember.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review in Canada?

No. Google's policy prohibits offering incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods in exchange for a review, or for changing or removing one. Reviews posted because of an incentive can be removed, and the profile can be restricted.

### Can I only ask happy customers for reviews?

No. Google does not allow businesses to discourage negative reviews or selectively ask satisfied customers for them. This is often called review gating. Ask every customer the same way.

### Can my employees leave Google reviews for my business?

Google treats current or former employment as a conflict of interest, so employee reviews of their own employer are not allowed on Maps. Canada's Competition Bureau has also warned that undisclosed employee reviews can make the business liable under the Competition Act.

### Do I need consent under CASL to email a customer asking for a review?

Treat a review request as a commercial electronic message and make sure you have consent. A recent purchase usually gives implied consent for two years. Every message must identify your business, give contact details, and include an unsubscribe option that works within 10 business days.
