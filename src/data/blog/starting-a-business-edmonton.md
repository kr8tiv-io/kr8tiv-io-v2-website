# Starting a business in Edmonton in 2026, step by step, with real fees

To start a business in Edmonton, you choose a structure, check and register your name with the Province, get a business number from the Canada Revenue Agency, confirm the zoning, and apply for a City of Edmonton business licence. A home-based business may also need a development permit. Most owners pay a few hundred dollars in government fees for all of it.

The official pages cover each step on its own. What they do not tell you is the order, or which mistakes cost money later. This is the order I would follow, with every fee and timeline checked against Province of Alberta, City of Edmonton and federal pages on 19 November 2026.

I am a brand and web designer, not a lawyer or an accountant. Where a decision has legal or tax consequences, I say so.

## Step 1. Decide the structure

The [Province of Alberta's business name page](https://www.alberta.ca/register-business-name) is blunt about the first choice. A trade name, also called a sole proprietorship, "does not have a legal existence in its own right." The owner is personally responsible for the debts of the business. A partnership shares profit and risk among the partners. A corporation is a separate legal entity.

The Province recommends getting legal advice if you are not sure which form suits you. I would add an accountant to that. The right answer depends on your income, your risk and whether you will have partners, and changing it later costs paperwork and fees.

## Step 2. Check the name before you fall for it

This is the step I see skipped most often, and it is the one that leads to a forced rebrand two years in.

Alberta's page says business names do not have to be unique. But if your name is the same as or similar to an existing business, corporation or trademark, the owners "could take your business to court," and you may have to change the name or pay damages. Registering a trade name does not give you any ownership of it.

So before you order cards or a sign, check:

1. **Similar registered names.** Alberta recommends a NUANS report, which lists similar business, corporation and trademark names. It is optional for a trade name but worth it.
2. **Trademarks.** Search the [Canadian Intellectual Property Office trademark database](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/srch). Look for similar names in your industry, not just exact matches.
3. **The domain.** Check the .ca and the .com. To hold a .ca, [CIRA's Canadian presence requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/resources/documents/domains/canadian-presence-requirements-registrants/) say you need to be, for example, a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, a Canadian corporation, or the owner of a trademark registered in Canada.
4. **Google.** Search the name plus "Edmonton" and plus "Alberta". If a business in the same trade already uses it, pick something else.
5. **Say it out loud.** Over the phone, on the radio, to a customer in a loud shop. If you have to spell it every time, that is a cost.

Two rules from the Province. A trade name cannot end in "Limited", "Incorporated" or "Corporation", or "Ltd.", "Inc." or "Corp.", because that would suggest a corporation. And a limited liability partnership must include "LLP".

If you want to know what turns a name into a brand people remember, I wrote about it in [logo or brand](/blog/logo-vs-brand-small-business/).

## Step 3. Register with the Province

A sole proprietor doing business under a name other than their own registers a trade name using a Declaration of Trade Name. You do this through an Alberta registry agent, who charges a government fee plus their own service fee. The service fee varies by agent, so it is worth calling two or three. A corporation registers through incorporation instead.

After registration, Alberta's page says a federal business number is emailed to you. Keep that email.

## Step 4. Sort out the CRA business number and GST

You need a [CRA business number](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/you-need-a-business-number-a-program-account.html) for GST/HST, payroll and other CRA accounts. You add those program accounts once you have the number.

On GST, the CRA's [small supplier rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) means you do not have to register until your taxable sales pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Many new businesses register early anyway, so they can claim back the GST on equipment and start-up costs. Ask your accountant which way suits you.

## Step 5. Confirm the zoning before you sign a lease

The City is direct on this. Its [business licence page](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/licences_permits/apply-for-a-business-licence) says to confirm with City staff that your activity is allowed at a location before you sign a lease, and that this due diligence is the owner's responsibility. If the zoning does not allow your use, rezoning "can be a long and complicated process."

You may also need a development or building permit if you change the use of a space (retail into a restaurant, say), renovate significantly, or put up a sign. The City lists signs on that page for a reason. My [Edmonton sign permit guide](/blog/edmonton-sign-permits/) has the 2026 fees.

The City runs a one-on-one Business-Friendly Support service for first- and second-time owners. Use it before you commit to a space.

## Step 6. Apply for the City business licence

You apply through the City's Self-Serve portal and choose one of three application types: Commercial, Home-Based, or Out of Town. You describe what the business does, and the City assigns licence categories. The City says you do not have to wait for development or building permits before applying, and encourages you to apply as soon as you have a location.

The fee depends on the tier of your category. From the City's [2026 business licence fee schedule](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/business-licence-fee-schedule-bylaw-20002.pdf):

| Tier | One-year licence | One-year renewal | Two-year licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | $63 | $53 | $116 |
| Tier 2 | $101 | $91 | $191 |
| Tier 3 | $271 | $244 | $520 |
| Tier 4 | $574 | $515 | $1,083 |
| Tier 5 | $728 | $659 | $1,387 |

Which tier are you? Schedule B of the [Business Licence Bylaw 20002](https://webdocs.edmonton.ca/bylaws/20002-Business-Licence-Bylaw-C-20250702.pdf) lists every category. General Business, Retail Sales (Minor), Restaurant or Food Service, Personal Service, and Construction, Contracting and Labour Service are all Tier 3. If more than one category applies, the highest tier sets the fee. Non-profits pay $48, and businesses based outside Edmonton pay an extra non-resident fee, listed at $489.

The renewal discount only applies if you renew on time.

## Step 7. If you work from home

Many Edmonton businesses start at the kitchen table, and the City treats that as a [home-based business](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/licences_permits/home-based-business). It needs a business licence like any other business. Businesses that are more visible, with customer visits or non-resident staff, also need a development permit. You can apply for both at once.

The 2026 development permit fee is $145 for a permitted home-based business or $415 for a discretionary one, which generally means one that needs a variance from the Zoning Bylaw. Some home-based businesses are exempt from the development permit entirely.

The City's rules for home-based businesses that need a permit include:

* No more than 2 employees on site at the same time who do not live there.
* An accessory building, such as a garage, used for the business is capped at 60 m².
* One storage trailer at most, no larger than 5.5 m by 2.6 m.
* No outdoor storage of commercial equipment, no outdoor business activity, and no outdoor speakers.
* Business vehicles of 4,600 kg gross weight or less can only be parked on site long enough to load or unload.

If you move house, you need a new development permit and licence.

## How long does it take?

The City publishes [processing times](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/processing-times). For July to September 2026, home-based business licences averaged 14 days against a 15-day target, with 79 per cent meeting it. Commercial licences averaged 17 days against a 25-day target. Out-of-town licences averaged 6 days. After approval, allow about two weeks for the licence certificate to arrive by mail.

## What to set up while you wait

The licence weeks are a good time to get the public side of the business ready:

* **A Google Business Profile**, in the exact legal or trade name. Read [why a Business Profile does not show](/blog/google-business-profile-not-showing/) before you start, because verification asks for the documents you are collecting now.
* **A simple website** with what you do, where, and how to reach you. It does not have to be big on day one. [What a website costs in Edmonton](/blog/website-cost-edmonton/) has real numbers.
* **A privacy policy and a consent checkbox** on any form that collects personal details. See [Alberta business website law](/blog/alberta-business-website-law/).
* **A logo, a colour and a typeface** that work on a truck, a sign and a phone screen.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a business licence in Edmonton in 2026?

It depends on the tier the City assigns to your business category. A one-year licence is $63 at Tier 1, $101 at Tier 2, $271 at Tier 3, $574 at Tier 4 and $728 at Tier 5. General business, small retail, restaurants, personal services and construction are listed at Tier 3.

### Do I need a business licence for a home-based business in Edmonton?

Yes. The City says home-based businesses must have a business licence like any other business, and many also need a development permit. The development permit fee is $145 for a permitted home-based business or $415 for a discretionary one, and you can apply for both together.

### Do I need to register my business name in Alberta?

If you run a sole proprietorship under a name other than your own legal name, you register it as a trade name through a registry agent. Registration does not give you ownership of the name, and duplicates can exist, so check business names and trademarks first.

### Do I need to charge GST when I start a business?

Not until your taxable sales pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. That is the small supplier threshold. You can register voluntarily before that, which can make sense if you buy a lot of equipment and want to claim the GST back.
