# Do you need a sign permit in Edmonton? Fees, exemptions and timelines for 2026

Yes, in most cases. The City of Edmonton says [all signs on private property need a sign permit](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/bylaws/permanent-signs) unless the Zoning Bylaw exempts them, and the exempt list is short. If your sign is lit, it also needs an electrical permit. Putting a sign up without the permit is an offence.

I design storefront signs for Edmonton businesses, and the permit is the step owners most often hear about too late. The fabricator is booked, the lease clock is running, and someone at the landlord's office asks for the permit number. This guide covers what the City actually asks for, what it costs this year, and how long it is taking. Every figure below was checked against City of Edmonton pages and the 2026 fee schedule on 19 November 2026.

## Which signs are exempt?

The exemptions live in [section 7.120 of the Zoning Bylaw](https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/part-7-administrative-and-interpretive-clauses/development-permits/7120-no-development-permit-required). In plain terms, you do not need a sign permit for:

* Address numbers.
* Signs inside the building that are not meant to be read from outside.
* A unlit fascia sign for a home-based business, no bigger than 0.3 m by 0.4 m.
* New copy on an existing, permitted sign, as long as the use, location, size, height, design and lighting stay the same.
* A unlit fascia sign of 0.5 m² or less in a non-residential zone.
* A small non-residential ground sign, up to 1.5 m high and 1.0 m² in area.
* A temporary window sign that stays up less than 30 days.
* One banner for an opening, a closing or a change of management, up to 5.0 m², for less than 30 days.
* Up to three temporary flag signs, each 2.0 m² or less.

That copy-change exemption is the most useful one for a rebrand. If you keep the existing cabinet and lighting and only replace the face, you may not need a new permit at all. Change the size or add lighting and you are back in the queue.

Half a square metre is smaller than people picture. It is roughly a 70 cm by 70 cm panel. Most fascia signs on a strip mall bay in Mill Woods or a shop on 124 Street are well past that.

## How much does a sign permit cost in 2026?

These are from the City's [2026 Planning and Development Fee Schedule](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/upe-planning-and-development-fee-schedules-consolidated.pdf), effective 1 January 2026.

| Sign type | 2026 City fee |
|---|---|
| Fascia sign (flat on the wall) | $195 |
| Freestanding, projecting, or a whole-site sign plan | $415 |
| Digital sign, minor or major | $960 per panel |
| Off-premises sign (advertising another business) | base fee plus 50% |
| Portable sign | $105 for 90 days, $310 for 365 days |

On top of the permit fee, the City adds a Safety Codes fee of 4 per cent of the permit cost, with a minimum of $4.50 and a cap of $560. A lit sign also carries its electrical permit.

If the sign hangs over a City sidewalk or road, you may need an encroachment agreement as well. The same schedule lists $300 for an encroachment application without circulation and $500 with circulation, and a $50 encroachment fee for aerial, canopy or projecting signs. Very small encroachments, under 0.05 m, carry no charge.

One small trap. On the City's permanent signs page, the link labelled "fee schedules" was pointing to the business licence fee document when I checked. The sign fees are in the Planning and Development schedule linked above.

## How long does the City take?

The City publishes [processing times](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/processing-times) every quarter. For July to September 2026:

| Permit | City target | Within target | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent sign | 5 days | 82% | 5 days |
| Digital sign | 55 days | 78% | 32 days |
| Portable sign | 5 days | 99% | under 1 day |

Five days is quick, but it is five days from a complete application. Missing engineering or a fuzzy site plan sends it back, and the clock starts again. Fabrication usually takes longer than the permit, so I tell clients to apply as soon as the design is approved, not after the sign is built.

## What do you need to apply?

You apply online through the City's [Self-Serve portal](https://selfserve.edmonton.ca/). The documents depend on the sign type, and the [permanent signs page](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/bylaws/permanent-signs) lists them:

**Fascia signs.** An elevation drawing or a photo of the whole building face, showing the sign's height, its clearance above grade, and the total area of all signs on that face. Plus a site plan.

**Engineering.** Fascia signs need stamped engineering under the APEGA standard, unless they fall into an exempt group: individual letters of 0.37 m² (about 4 sq ft) or less each, plywood signs of 3.0 m² or less, and cloth banners or painted signs.

**Freestanding signs.** A plot plan prepared by an Alberta Land Surveyor, so the City can see setbacks and property lines.

**Projecting signs.** Any sign that sticks out more than 0.4 m from the wall needs engineering.

**Digital signs.** The City treats a sign as minor digital if each message holds for at least 6 seconds with no animation. Anything livelier is major digital. A traffic safety study may be required, and brightness is capped at 400 nits. A sign near a provincial highway also needs a permit from Alberta Transportation.

**Electrical.** A lit sign gets an electrical permit added to the application. A licensed electrical contractor, or a certified sign installer (SSIT), can do the connection. A new electrical service needs an electrical contractor.

If you get stuck, the City's sign team answers at permanentsigns@edmonton.ca, or through 311.

## How big can my sign be?

The size and placement rules are in [section 6.90 of the Zoning Bylaw](https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/part-6-specific-development-regulations/690-signs), and they change by zone. A few that catch people:

* **Main streets and mixed-use zones** (MU, MUN, CN and similar): each fascia sign can be up to 20 m², and all fascia and flat projecting signs together cannot cover more than half of the building face. Signs stay below the eave on one- or two-storey buildings.
* **Residential zones:** a home-based business fascia sign cannot be lit and tops out at 1.0 m². Other permitted signs in these zones max out at 3.0 m², with external lighting only.
* **Clearance:** a sign over a walkway needs 2.5 m of clearance underneath. Over a fire access route, 5.0 m.
* **Lighting near homes:** a lit sign facing a residential property has to be off between midnight and 5 a.m., unless the business is open.
* **Digital brightness:** no more than 400 nits at night, and no more than 32 nits above ambient light.
* **Downtown core:** no off-premises or digital signs in the civic-centre area bounded by 105 Avenue, the river, 97 Street and 100 Street.

Old Strathcona has its own design guide on top of the bylaw, because much of Whyte Avenue sits in a historic area. If your shop is there, read it before you brief a designer. Heritage districts usually favour painted, projecting or individual-letter signs over a lit box.

## Can a grant pay for the sign?

There are two City programs worth knowing. Both reimburse you after the work is done, and neither guarantees funding.

**The Storefront Refresh and Signage Grant.** It covers 50 per cent of eligible costs, up to $1,000 per ground-floor, street-facing unit, for a new or refreshed fascia or projecting sign. Billboards and digital signs are out. It is first come, first served, the work has to be permitted, and it has to be finished within six months. The program contact is storefront.improvement@edmonton.ca or 780-221-2337. The details are on the City's [business improvement area grants page](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/business_resources/business-improvement-area-grants).

**The Storefront Improvement Program.** This is the bigger one: a 50 per cent matching reimbursement up to $50,000, for buildings in a Business Improvement Area or a target area. A sign alone does not qualify. You need one "critical" improvement, such as windows or the facade, plus two others, and a new fascia sign or sign infrastructure can count as one of the others. The building must be at least ten years old and mostly commercial. Home-based businesses are not eligible, and vinyl wraps, digital signs, billboards and sandwich boards are not covered. Design and engineering fees are eligible costs, which matters if you are paying someone like me to draw it.

The timing is the hard part. Intake for 2026 ran in February, April and November, and the November window closes on 30 November 2026. The 2027 intakes are planned for February, April and September. The program guide says review takes about six weeks, and work cannot start before approval. If you are planning a spring storefront, apply in the February window.

I cover what the money is and is not good for in the [Alberta website and storefront grants reality check](/blog/alberta-website-grants/).

## What I would do, in order

1. Check the zone of your address on the City's map, then read the matching part of section 6.90.
2. Ask the landlord for any sign criteria in the lease. Many plazas have their own rules on colour, lighting and size, and they are stricter than the City's.
3. Design the sign to the measured facade, not a photo. Get the total sign area on that face.
4. Decide on lighting early. It changes the permit, the electrical work, and the cost.
5. If a grant fits, apply for it before anything is ordered.
6. Apply for the permit when the design is approved. Book fabrication around the permit, not the other way round.

This is a practical guide, not a ruling. The Development Officer decides, and bylaws change. Check the linked City pages before you submit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a sign on my building in Edmonton?

Almost certainly. The City says every sign on private property needs a sign permit unless it is listed as exempt in section 7.120 of the Zoning Bylaw. The exempt list is short and mostly covers small, unlit or temporary signs.

### How much does a sign permit cost in Edmonton in 2026?

The 2026 fee schedule lists $195 for a fascia sign, $415 for a freestanding or projecting sign, and $960 per panel for a digital sign. Off-premises signs add 50 per cent. A Safety Codes fee of 4 per cent is added on top.

### How long does an Edmonton sign permit take?

For July to September 2026, the City reported an average of 5 days for permanent sign permits and 32 days for digital signs. Incomplete applications take longer, so the drawings and engineering need to be right the first time.

### Can a grant pay for a new storefront sign in Edmonton?

Sometimes. The Storefront Refresh and Signage Grant reimburses half of eligible costs, up to $1,000 per ground-floor unit, and the larger Storefront Improvement Program can cover sign work as part of a bigger facade project. Both have rules on location, timing and permits.
