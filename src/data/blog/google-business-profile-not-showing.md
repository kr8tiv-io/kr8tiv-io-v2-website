# Why is my Google Business Profile not showing? A fix-it guide for Edmonton businesses

When a Google Business Profile does not show up, it is almost always one of four things. It is not verified yet. It has been suspended. There is a duplicate listing competing with it. Or it is live, and other businesses are simply ranking above it for the search you typed.

Those four problems have very different fixes, and the most common mistake I see is treating them as one. Owners in Edmonton delete and recreate a profile because they think it is broken, when it was just sitting on page two of the map pack. That can make things worse.

Everything below comes from Google's own Business Profile help pages, checked on 19 November 2026. Google changes these pages often, so I have linked each one.

## First, find out which problem you have

Do these three checks from a phone, signed out of your Google account, or in a private browser window. Signed in as the owner, Google may show you your own profile when nobody else sees it.

**Check 1. Search your exact business name plus "Edmonton".** If the profile shows up, it exists and is public. Your problem is ranking, and you can skip to the ranking section.

**Check 2. Sign in to your Business Profile and look at the status.** It will tell you if the profile is unverified, pending, suspended or restricted.

**Check 3. Search your phone number and your street address.** If a second listing appears with your name, an old name, or a previous tenant's name, you have a duplicate problem.

## Problem 1. The profile is not verified

An unverified profile may not show publicly at all. Google [says verification can take up to 5 business days](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15698162?hl=en) once you submit it.

You do not get to choose the method. [Google decides which verification options you see](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en), based on the business type, the information on the profile, and other factors. The options can include a phone call or text, email, or a recorded video. Two practical notes:

* If the phone number goes to an automated menu, you will not get the code. Point the profile at a line a person or a mobile can answer.
* Video verification is the one most Edmonton owners now see. Google's [video verification rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en) are strict. The video has to be recorded live through the Business Profile on a phone, in one unedited take of at least 30 seconds. You cannot upload a clip you recorded earlier. For a storefront, Google wants the location (street and signage), proof the business exists, and proof you manage it, such as opening a cash register, a storage room or a point-of-sale system. For a service-area business, it suggests nearby street signs, your tools or a branded vehicle, and documents such as a business permit, an invoice or a utility bill. Do not show other people's faces, bank details or ID numbers.

For a storefront in a strip mall on 137 Avenue, that means starting outside on the sign and the unit number, walking in, and opening something only staff can open. For a home-based tradesperson in Mill Woods, it means the street sign at the corner, the truck with the business name, the tools and your City business licence, without filming the inside of your house more than you need to.

## Problem 2. The profile is suspended or restricted

A suspended profile does not show on Maps or Search. Google's guidelines are long, but most suspensions I have looked at trace back to three rules in the [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en):

* **The name must match the real-world name.** It should be what customers see on your signage, website and stationery. Adding "Best Plumber Edmonton" to the name is the classic trigger.
* **One profile per business.** Not one per service, and not one per suburb.
* **Use the fewest categories that describe the core business.**

If a profile is suspended, [Google's appeal process](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) asks for evidence. That can include your business registration, business licence, tax certificate or a utility bill. The name and address on the documents should match the profile. In Edmonton, that usually means your City of Edmonton business licence and your Alberta trade name registration or articles of incorporation. If you do not have those yet, my [guide to starting a business in Edmonton](/blog/starting-a-business-edmonton/) covers both.

Two warnings from Google's page. You can only submit an appeal once per decision, so put all the evidence in the first time. And do not create a new profile while you wait. A new profile for the same business is a duplicate.

Restrictions are different. Google can [restrict a profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en) after it detects policy-violating reviews. That can block new reviews, unpublish existing ones, or show a warning banner to customers. If you have been buying reviews or giving discounts for them, stop, and read [how to get Google reviews legally in Canada](/blog/google-reviews-canada-rules/).

## Problem 3. A duplicate is splitting your listing

Google says [duplicate profiles will not show on Search or Maps](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178?hl=en). Duplicates usually appear when:

* A previous owner, an old agency or a family member set one up and nobody remembers the login.
* You moved, and someone created a new profile instead of editing the address. Google says to edit the address on the existing profile.
* Two businesses share one address, such as a salon and a booth renter. Google expects distinct businesses at the same address to have distinct signage.

If you find an old profile you cannot access, you can request ownership from within Google's tools. Google's page says the current owner gets 3 days to respond before you can take further steps.

## Problem 4. It is live, but it is not ranking

This is the most common case. Google's [local ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says results are based mainly on **relevance**, **distance** and a third factor it calls popularity in one line and **prominence** in the next. It also says plainly that "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking."

**Relevance** is how well your profile matches the search. If you are listed as "General contractor" but people search "basement development", you are less relevant than a business listed and described for basements.

**Distance** is distance from the searcher. This is the one you cannot change. If you are in Windermere, you will struggle to appear for someone searching from Clareview, no matter how good the profile is. That is normal, not broken.

**Prominence** is how well-known the business is. Google says it draws on information such as how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help.

So the practical fixes are:

1. **Pick the right primary category.** Search the term your customers use, look at the top three profiles, and see what categories they use.
2. **Fill in everything that is true.** Hours, holiday hours, services, attributes, photos of the real place and the real work.
3. **Get reviews steadily, and legally.** Ask every customer, not just the happy ones.
4. **Make your website agree with your profile.** Same name, same address, same phone, and a page for each main service. A website with one vague "services" page gives Google little to match against.
5. **Get mentioned locally.** Your BIA directory, the Edmonton Chamber of Commerce if you are a member, supplier "where to buy" pages and local news all count as signals that you exist.

## Service-area businesses: why your address is hidden

If you go to customers rather than having them come to you, Google wants you to [set up as a service-area business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) and hide your address. A hybrid business without permanent signage should list as a service-area business too.

The rules that matter in the Edmonton region:

* You can add up to 20 service areas, by city or postal code. You cannot draw a radius.
* Google says the areas should generally not extend further than about 2 hours' drive from where you are based.
* Changes to service areas can take up to 48 hours to appear.

A realistic list for an Edmonton-based trade might be Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Stony Plain, Leduc, Beaumont and Fort Saskatchewan. Listing every town in Alberta does not help you rank further away. Your profile still competes from where you actually are.

## Edits are not showing

Google says profile edits [usually appear within about 10 minutes](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en), but some can take up to 30 days to review. Name, category and address changes are the ones most likely to be held. Make one change at a time, and do not keep re-saving it.

## What I would not do

* Do not stuff keywords into the business name.
* Do not list a virtual office or a mailbox. Google's guidelines say a rented mailing address you do not operate from is not eligible, and a co-working desk only counts if it has clear signage and is staffed and open to customers during business hours.
* Do not make a second profile for a second service.
* Do not pay anyone who promises a guaranteed top-three map position. Google says it cannot be bought.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my Google Business Profile not showing up on Google Maps?

The usual causes are that the profile is not verified yet, it has been suspended, it is a duplicate of another listing, or it is live but ranking below other businesses for the search you tried. Search for your exact business name first. If it appears, the profile exists and the problem is ranking.

### How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

Google says verification can take up to 5 business days after you submit. Profile edits usually appear within about 10 minutes, but Google says some can take up to 30 days to review.

### Can I pay Google to rank my Business Profile higher?

No. Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence. Ads can appear above the map results, but they are labelled as ads and do not change your organic position.

### Should I create a new profile if mine is suspended?

No. Google tells owners not to create a new profile while appealing. Gather proof such as your business licence, registration and a utility bill that match the name and address on the profile, then submit one appeal. Google allows only one appeal per decision.
