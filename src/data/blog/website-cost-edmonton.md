# How much does a website cost in Edmonton in 2026?

A small business website in Edmonton costs somewhere between about $500 and $5,000 to build, depending on what is in it, and then something every month to keep it running. That is a wide range, so this post narrows it with prices that Edmonton studios and the big builders publish themselves. I checked every figure on 19 November 2026.

I run KR8TIV, a design studio in Edmonton, so I have a horse in this race. My own prices are at the end, and I have put them next to the competition rather than hiding them.

The pattern is clear. A small brochure site with a handful of pages starts around $500 to $1,200. An online store starts around $2,000 to $4,500. ## What does a do-it-yourself builder cost?

Building it yourself on a platform swaps the build fee for a subscription and your own evenings.

**Shopify.** On [Shopify's Canadian pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing), Basic is $49 CAD a month on monthly billing, or $37 a month billed yearly. Online card rates on Basic start at 2.8% plus 30 cents. Grow and Advanced cost more, with lower card rates.

**Squarespace.** The [Squarespace pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing) lists Basic at CA$30 a month on monthly billing, and showed CA$21 a month on an annual promotion when I checked. Its card processing rate on that plan is 2.9% plus CA$0.30. Promotions change often, so check the live page.

**Wix.** Wix showed me location-based prices that looked like US dollars from my server, so I am not quoting them here. Check the page from your own browser.

A builder is a good choice for plenty of businesses. I wrote about when in [template or custom website](/blog/template-vs-custom-website/). The real cost is time. If you bill $80 an hour and lose 25 hours to fighting a template, that is $2,000 you did not see on an invoice.

## What makes one website cost more than another?

Here are the things that move a quote, roughly in order of how much they move it.

**Who writes the words.** Copy is labour. A studio that writes your pages from an interview will charge more than one that pastes in what you send.

**How many different page layouts there are.** Ten pages that share one layout cost less than five pages that each need their own design. A services page, a project gallery and a booking page are three different jobs.

**Selling online.** Products, variants, shipping zones, taxes and payment set-up add real hours. Ambrite's page makes a useful point here: customers in Alberta pay GST only, but selling elsewhere in Canada generally means charging the GST or HST rate for where the order is delivered.

**Bookings, quotes and integrations.** A form that emails you is simple. A form that books into a calendar, checks a service area and sends the lead into a CRM is not.

**Photography.** Stock photos are cheap and look it. A half day with a photographer in your actual shop costs more and does more.

**Search work.** A site that is built to be found, with page titles, service-area pages and Google Business Profile set-up, takes longer than one that only looks good. More on what that costs in [SEO costs in Edmonton](/blog/seo-cost-edmonton/).

**Revisions and meetings.** Two rounds of changes is common. Unlimited rounds is a red flag on either side.

## What does a website cost every month?

This is the part quotes leave out. Every live website carries some combination of:

* **A domain name.** A yearly fee for your .ca or .com.
* **Hosting or a builder plan.** $20 a month at Agency7 if they host, $49 a month for Shopify Basic on monthly billing, CA$30 for Squarespace Basic.
* **Payment fees.** Usually a percentage of each sale plus a fixed amount per transaction.
* **Changes and care.** Edmonton studios publish care plans between about $49 and $249 a month. KR8TIV's Website Care is $75 a month.
* **Apps and plug-ins.** Booking tools, review widgets and email marketing are often their own subscriptions.

Add up the first three years, not just the build. A $500 site with $100 a month in fees and plug-ins costs $4,100 over three years. A $2,000 site on $30 a month hosting costs $3,080. Neither is wrong. They are different decisions.

## What about GST?

Most studios add 5 per cent GST. Agency7's page says so plainly. Under the [Canada Revenue Agency's small supplier rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), a business only has to register for GST once its taxable sales pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. A smaller studio that is under the threshold does not charge it. KR8TIV does not charge GST at the moment.

If you are GST registered yourself, you can usually claim back the GST you pay as an input tax credit, so it matters less to you. If you are not, a quote with GST added costs you 5 per cent more than it looks. Compare like with like.

## How do I tell a fair quote from a padded one?

Ask for the quote in writing, broken into lines. Then check:

1. **Which pages are included, by name.** "Up to 5 pages" is fine if you know which five.
2. **Who writes the copy and supplies the photos.**
3. **How many rounds of changes.** And what one more round costs.
4. **Who owns the domain, the site and the files.** The domain should be registered in your name, on your account. Always.
5. **What the monthly cost is after launch,** and whether you can move the site if you leave.
6. **What "SEO" means in the quote.** If it is one line with no detail, it is probably page titles and a sitemap. That is fine, but it is not a search plan.
7. **Whether GST is included.**

Red flags I see often: a guaranteed number-one ranking (Google says [nobody can guarantee one](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo)), a domain registered to the agency, no written scope, and a monthly fee with no explanation of what it pays for.

## So what should you budget?

My honest ranges, based on the published prices above and the jobs I quote:

| What you need | Realistic Edmonton budget |
|---|---|
| A single page that says who you are and takes enquiries | $500 to $1,250 |
| A small service-business site, five to eight pages, with copy written for you | $1,500 to $4,000 |
| An online store with a modest catalogue | $2,000 to $6,000 |
| A large site with bookings, integrations or many locations | Quoted after a scope call, often well above $6,000 |

Then add a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars a year to keep it running.

Before you spend any of it, read [what makes a website earn its keep](/blog/website-that-earns-its-keep-alberta/). A cheaper site that answers the customer's real question will beat an expensive one that does not.
