# How a Google Business Profile helps an Edmonton trades business get found

A Google Business Profile is the card that can appear when someone in Edmonton or elsewhere in Alberta searches for a local service and looks at the map. It is not a second website. It is the public listing that helps people confirm you exist, see what you do, and choose whether to call, visit a site, or read a review.

If you are a plumber, roofer, blaster, scaffold crew or other mobile trade, this often matters more than another blog post about “brand awareness.” A lot of hiring starts with a short local search. Without a profile, you are asking that person to already know your name.

KR8TIV is an Edmonton studio, and we have a profile of our own. This guide is the practical version of what we would tell a trades client before anyone starts arguing about categories. It is not a promise that a listing will put you in the map pack.

This is also not a paid-ads article. If you are planning a media test, use the guide to [local advertising in Edmonton](/blog/local-advertising-edmonton-alberta/). A profile and an ad campaign can support each other. They are not the same spend.

## What does the profile actually do?

It tells Google, and the person searching, the basics: your name, category, service area or address, hours, phone number, website, photos, and reviews. Google’s own [Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/) is the rulebook for what belongs in each field.

When those basics are complete and match the real business, you have a chance to appear in local results. When they are missing, duplicated, or dressed up with leftover keywords in the name, you give Google and customers a messy story.

A fictional Fort Saskatchewan welder who lists the company as “Best Welding Edmonton Sherwood Park Mobile Fabrication Ltd.” is not being clever. Google’s guidelines ask for the real-world name, the one on the truck and the invoice. Put the services in the services fields, and the places you work in the service area.

The listing also gives you a place for reviews that a new customer can read in the same moment they find you. In a crowded trade, that social proof often does more than a poetic description.

## Storefront or service area: which one is honest?

This is the decision a lot of Alberta trades get stuck on, because many of us work from a shop, a yard, or a home office we do not want on the internet.

A storefront profile makes sense when customers can arrive at a real, staffed location: a showroom, a counter, a yard with signage. A service-area profile makes sense when you go to the customer and do not want, or are not allowed, to publish that address.

Be honest. If you hide the address, you may appear less often for some “near me” searches than a visible shop. That is a trade-off, not a trick to work around with a fake downtown unit. Google’s rules are also clear about creating locations that do not exist.

Write the service area as the places you actually take jobs. Edmonton plus Sherwood Park, St. Albert and Spruce Grove is a real pattern for a lot of crews. “All of Canada” is usually not, unless you genuinely take that work and can say how.

Then match the website. If the listing says you serve greater Edmonton and the homepage implies a national company with local offices you do not have, both pages get harder to trust. A [clear website](/services/web-design-edmonton/) should repeat the same name, number, hours and coverage the profile uses.

## What is worth setting up well on day one?

Do the unglamorous things first. They are the difference between a listing that can work and a listing that is just sitting there.

* Use the real business name, and the category that best describes the work you want more of.
* Add the services you actually sell, in ordinary language.
* Publish a phone number you answer, written the same way it appears on the site.
* Link to the most useful website page, usually the homepage or the main service page.
* Add photographs of real work, the crew, or the shop, with permission. A profile full of generic stock rooms helps no one.
* Set hours you can keep. “Open 24 hours” is only impressive if it is true.
* Turn on messaging only if someone will reply.

Then look at the listing as if you have never heard of yourself. Would you call? Would you know what happens next? If the only photo is a blurry van door, fix that before you worry about weekly posts on the profile.

Reviews are the long game. Ask a customer after a job you are proud of, send the official review link, and leave it alone. A short note is enough. “If you were happy with the eavestrough work, a Google review helps other people in Edmonton find us” is clearer than a paragraph about algorithms.

Never buy reviews, never write them for your own business, and never trade them in a way that breaks Google’s rules. One fake review can cost more trust than it buys. If a review is genuinely fake, use the official tools to flag it and reply calmly if it stays visible for a while.

## How do you know whether the listing is helping?

Watch the questions people ask when they call. “I found you on Google” is useful. So is “I saw the photos of the fence job.” Keep a simple log rather than treating every dashboard click as a booked job.

Google’s profile insights can show calls, website taps and direction requests. They cannot tell you, by themselves, whether those were qualified jobs in your service area. Compare them with your own enquiry notes.

If the listing exists and nobody can find a matching website, or the website uses a different name, fix that first. Local search is partly a consistency problem. It is not a place to invent a longer company title for keyword coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a Google Business Profile if I already have a website?

A website and a profile do different jobs. The profile is often what helps you appear in Google’s local and map results. The website is where a serious buyer confirms the service and sends an enquiry. They should match, and the profile should link to the site.

### Should a mobile Edmonton trades business show its home address?

Not automatically. Many service-area businesses hide the address and list the areas they actually serve. That can be the honest setup. It can also mean you appear less often for some “near me” searches than a visible shopfront. Choose the option that matches how you work, then keep it consistent.

### Will a Google listing guarantee I rank for plumber Edmonton or roofer near me?

No. A complete, accurate profile is the starting point for local visibility, not a promised placement. Categories, reviews, distance, relevance and the quality of your information all play a part, and Google can change how results appear.

### How do I ask customers in Alberta for a Google review?

Ask after a job you are proud of, make it easy with the official review link, and accept that some people will not do it. A short, specific request works better than a generic plea. Do not buy reviews or write them yourself.

## Make the listing match the business

The profile cannot invent a reputation, and it cannot replace a site that explains the work. It can make a real Edmonton or Alberta trades business easier to find and easier to trust at the moment someone is looking.
