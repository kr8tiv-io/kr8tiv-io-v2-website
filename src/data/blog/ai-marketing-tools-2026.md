# Which AI marketing tools are worth paying for in 2026

A founder in Edmonton does not lose the week to a blank page. They lose it to eleven subscriptions that all promised to fix marketing. Most of them are an older product with a chat box on the side. The work still sounds like everyone else, and the card still gets billed.

The shift this year is not a new marketing platform. The frontier assistants got good enough to do the daily pile: the brief, the first draft, the competitor summary, the twelve headlines, the email you were avoiding. The specialised tools still have jobs. They come second, and only when you can name the job.

This is a filter for owner-run companies in Edmonton, across Alberta, and the rest of Canada. No twelve-person marketing department assumed. Prices below are US list prices before tax, checked against vendor pages and help centres on 17 November 2026. If the checkout disagrees, the checkout is right. A Canadian card may also add a conversion fee. KR8TIV's own floors, further down, are in CAD.

## Which assistant should a founder pay for?

Pay for one assistant and use it until you can feel where it is lying. A second assistant is a comparison, not a personality.

| Job this month | Tool | US list price, 17 Nov 2026 | Leave it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily drafts, briefs, ad lines | ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro | $20 a month | You will not edit what comes back |
| Lighter ChatGPT use | ChatGPT Go | $8 a month in the US | You need the deeper work Plus is for |
| Heavy all-day use | ChatGPT Pro | From $100 a month | You write a few pages a week. Pro is the wrong size |
| The company already lives in Google | Gemini's paid plans | About $5 to $20 | You only wanted a second place to paste the same brief |
| Cited research | Perplexity Pro | $20 a month, or $200 a year | You needed a summary of a page you already had open |
| A bot that keeps working | X Subscription Premium | $30 a month, or $300 a year | You only wanted chat. Plus at $8 does not include Grok Bot |

OpenAI's own help centre still prices [ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus). Go is the lighter plan, [$8 a month in the US](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-go/), with local prices in some countries. Pro starts at $100 and is for people who live in the tool. A founder writing offers, emails, and a homepage does not need it.

Claude Pro is the other $20 plan, or $17 a month if you pay $200 for the year, on [Claude's pricing page](https://claude.com/pricing). Max is $100 a month. Ultra is $200. I reach for Pro when the draft has to stay in a voice for more than a paragraph, or when the source is a long brief, a transcript, or a messy export. The higher plans buy volume, not taste.

ChatGPT remains the broad desk: images, files, a pile of variants, and enough general knowledge to get a first pass out before the coffee cools. Gemini fits when the truth of the business already sits in Google Docs, Gmail, and the Business Profile. Google's individual paid plans have been listed this month around $4.99 and $19.99 in the US. The names move. Open Google's plan page before you save a card.

Perplexity Pro, at $20 a month or $200 a year, is the research bench. It is useful when you want sources attached to the answer, which a normal chat still treats as optional. It is not a writer, and it is not a reason to stop reading the page it cited.

None of them know your customer. They accelerate the person who already knows what the company should sound like, and what it should refuse to say. If that person does not exist yet, a model will invent a brochure voice and defend it. The longer version of that trap is [how to use AI without looking like everyone else](/blog/ai-creativity-small-business-edmonton/).

## Is a copy tool worth it, or is Jasper just a chatbot with a brand kit?

For one founder, Jasper is the expensive way to do what Claude or ChatGPT already does.

[Jasper's pricing page](https://www.jasper.ai/pricing), read on 17 November 2026, lists Pro at $69 a seat each month, or $59 a seat billed yearly. That seat includes two Brand Voices, five knowledge assets, and three audiences. Business is a custom quote, and that is where the GEO and research agents sit. There is a seven-day trial. There is no cheap solo plan on that page.

Jasper earns the seat when several people publish in one stored voice, every week, and nobody wants to keep the voice in a document they forget to paste. It does not write better sentences than the frontier models. Independent reviews this autumn keep landing on the same split: teams buy the system, individuals buy the $20 assistant.

A stored brand voice is also only as good as the samples you fed it. If those samples are generic, the voice will be generic at a higher monthly rate. Write the refusal list yourself. The words you will not say. The claim you cannot prove this month. Paste that into whichever assistant you already pay for. You have just built the part of Jasper that matters, without the seat.

## Do you still need SEO tools, and what about AI answers?

Yes for the old job. Not yet for the new dashboard.

Semrush and Ahrefs still matter when you want keyword data, a crawl, and a look at who links to you. They do not become optional because a chatbot can invent a content calendar. A calendar of pages nobody asked for is how a site gets full and stays invisible.

The new job is answer visibility. Someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews who to call for a thing in Edmonton, and your name is either in the answer or it is not. People call this GEO, which is a grand name for a simple question: when a model has to recommend someone, does it have a page of yours worth repeating?

Google's guide to [AI features in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there is no special trick, and no special schema, required to be eligible. The page has to be indexed, and eligible to show a snippet. Their note on [generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) is the other half. Mass-produced pages with little original value can count as spam. One useful page that a person checked does not. The model predicts a likely sentence. Likely, for a local company, is "passionate team serving greater Edmonton." That sentence is how you become invisible in both the old results and the new ones.

Trackers showed up to measure the new surface. Semrush sells an AI Visibility Toolkit as a paid add-on, published near $99 a month for a small set of prompts in November 2026 comparisons of their own page. Extra prompts cost more. Profound, the specialist, offers brands a short trial and then a custom quote. Agency plans are priced like agency plans. A founder with one city and one offer does not need either on day one.

Buy a tracker after you have something to track:

- One page that names the offer, who it is for, and who it is not for.
- Proof you can stand behind this month. A method, a photo of the real work, a number you measured.
- The same words on the homepage and on the Google Business Profile, so the map and the answer are not telling two stories.
- Internal links to the service a person can actually buy. A blog that never points at a next step is a diary.

That is the same standard as [a blog that brings leads](/blog/blog-that-ranks-and-brings-leads/). GEO does not replace it. For an Alberta company, local search and the AI answer now sit in the same decision. The customer still calls one number. Rankings without a page that can take the call are a vanity metric with better lighting.

## Which creative and ad tools are worth the card?

Canva, once the brand already exists. A video tool, once the line is true. A specialised ad generator, only if you will pay for the re-rolls in time as well as credits.

Canva Pro is the practical bench for social crops, simple ads, and decks, and Magic Studio is the AI layer inside it rather than a separate product. US listings this autumn put Pro at $18 a month, or $144 a year, for one person. Canva moved that annual price during 2026, so read [Canva's pricing page](https://www.canva.com/pricing/) at checkout. Free is enough to learn the editor. Pro is the point where brand kits and the heavier AI tools stop feeling rationed. Canva will not invent a distinctive identity. It will help you repeat one you already have. If you do not have one, you will repeat Canva.

Avatar and voice tools are cheap next to a shoot and expensive next to a bad script. HeyGen's creator plan has been listed around $29 a month. ElevenLabs sits in the tens of dollars for a paid voice plan, and the licence has to allow the ad you want to run. Both will make something that looks finished. Finished is not the same as believed. If you would not say the line at the counter, do not put a generated face on it.

Credit-based ad generators, Higgsfield among them, moved their plan names more than once this year. Budget the re-rolls. A thirty-second ad is several clips, and the first render is rarely the one you ship. The human work is the product, the offer, and the cut. The tool is the camera you rent.

Two filters before any of it becomes an ad, the same ones that show up in [local advertising in Edmonton](/blog/local-advertising-edmonton-alberta/):

- Speed. On Whyte or 124 Street, someone can stop. On the Whitemud or the Henday, they will not. If the idea needs a paragraph, it is a page, not an ad.
- Dark. After about 4:30 in November, a lot of Edmonton sees the work as a small bright rectangle, or a sign in headlights. If the clever version dies there, ship the plain one.

A responsive search ad still mixes your lines. Headlines have to stand alone, under 30 characters, and descriptions under 90. Ask the assistant for fifteen, using only facts you typed. Keep the ones you would say to a person who is about to pay you. The build of a full campaign is in [the campaign note](/blog/marketing-campaign-edmonton/). Ads and acquisition setup at KR8TIV starts from $900 CAD. Ad spend is paid by you, to the platform. No one honest guarantees the leads.

## Can an agent run the marketing?

It can run a narrow job. It cannot run the company.

This is the part of 2026 that is actually new. A Grok Bot is an assistant with a computer in the cloud. xAI's own guide says the bot keeps working while the laptop is closed, can use connected apps, and can call other tools through MCP, which is the plain standard for letting a model reach a service instead of guessing. Grok Bot is included on X Subscription Premium, Super, and Ultra. It is not included on the $8 Plus plan. Premium is $30 a month, or $300 a year, on [the X Subscription pricing page](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/pricing). Existing SuperGrok plans continue. New buyers are choosing from this list, not from the old name.

The official pattern, and the only one I trust, is a bot with one job and an approval rule on anything you cannot undo. Research overnight. Draft the Monday brief. Pull the questions customers asked last week. Prepare twelve headlines and stop. Sending, publishing, and spending get an ask-first rule. If a bot can buy ads on a saved card, you do not have an employee. You have an accident with a budget.

MCP is not a product you subscribe to. It is a connection. The same idea shows up in Claude, in ChatGPT's connectors, and in a small automation tool such as n8n or Zapier when you want a model to run on a schedule and drop the result in a doc. One workflow, one output, one person who reads it. A stack of agents that message each other is how a founder ends up managing software instead of customers.

HubSpot and Salesforce will sell you an agent inside the CRM. Those make sense when the pipeline, the fields, and the follow-up already exist, and a team is drowning in steps they have done a hundred times. They are a strange first purchase for a company that still keeps leads in a notebook. The notebook, updated, beats a chatbot bolted to an empty database.

## What about email and the CRM?

Write the email in the assistant you already pay for. Send it from the tool that already has permission to reach your list.

HubSpot's free CRM is the usual step up from a spreadsheet, and it is enough until the pipeline is real. The AI features inside HubSpot, and the agent products attached to larger CRMs, assume you have contacts, a stage for each deal, and a next action. If those are missing, the AI writes a nurture sequence for people you cannot name.

A shop with orders can stay in the email tool attached to the store. A simple list can stay in Mailchimp or whatever already sends the note. Do not add a second sender so an AI can "own the journey." Two tools means two lists, and two lists means someone gets the wrong email, or no email, which is a brand problem wearing a software costume.

The useful AI job in a CRM is small. Summarise the last call. Draft the follow-up in the customer's words. Flag the quote that has sat for seven days. A person still decides what goes out. Paste nothing private into a public model that you would not want read back to you: a customer's address, an unreleased price, a quote, a list export. That is not research. That is a leak.

## What an Alberta founder should buy first

If you only change one thing this month, change this, in order:

1. One assistant. ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro, $20 US. Use it on real work for two weeks. Keep a note of what you had to delete, because that note is your brand voice.
2. A page that says the true thing. Offer, who it is for, proof, next step. The assistant may draft it. You publish the cut. If the site and the Google listing disagree, fix that before you buy a tracker.
3. Canva Pro, only if you are the person shipping the social posts and the brand system already exists.
4. Grok Bot on Premium, only if you have a repeating job with a written stop rule. Research and drafts. Not send, not spend, not publish.
5. A GEO tracker or Jasper, later, when the failure is measurement or a team, not a missing point of view.

Skip the rest until one of those five is hurting. A new subscription is a pleasant way to avoid the sentence you have not written.

Forty-five minutes, if you want the drill instead of the shopping list. Write the offer in one sentence. Ask your assistant for twelve headlines a stranger can believe alone. Delete any line you would not say at the counter. Put the survivor on the homepage, the Google Business Profile, and one ad. If you cannot write the sentence, stop buying tools. A model will invent a company for you, and you will spend the winter sounding like a template.

## What still has to be a person

AI is excellent at speed, volume, summaries, and the ugly first draft. It is weak at an original point of view, at consistent taste, and at the specific reason a customer in St. Albert, Sherwood Park, or Calgary should call this company and not the next one. Generic output is cheaper than it has ever been. Distinctive work is not.
