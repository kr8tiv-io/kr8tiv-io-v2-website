# Are there grants for a small business website in Alberta? An honest 2026 check

Short answer: not right now. As of 19 November 2026, I could not find an open federal or Alberta program that pays directly for a small business to get a website built. The best-known one, the Canada Digital Adoption Program, ended in March 2025. Alberta Innovates closed its Digital Traction program in May 2026.

That is not the answer people want, and plenty of pages still say otherwise. Some are out of date. Some are written by agencies who would like you to believe a grant will pay their invoice. I checked each program below on its official page on 19 November 2026, and I have said plainly which ones are closed.

There is still money around. It just pays for different things: training, storefronts, and tech products. Here is what is open, what is closed, and what each one actually covers.

## Closed: the Canada Digital Adoption Program (CDAP)

CDAP was the program behind most "get a grant for your website" advice in 2022 and 2023. It had two streams:

* **Grow Your Business Online**, with grants of up to $2,400 toward adopting e-commerce and other digital tools.
* **Boost Your Business Technology**, which covered up to 90 per cent of the cost of a digital adoption plan from an approved advisor, to a maximum of $15,000.

Both are finished. The federal government's [ISED Info Source update](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/atip-services/en/ised-info-source-updates/info-source-2025-update) says both streams concluded on 31 March 2025. [BDC's CDAP page](https://www.bdc.ca/en/canada-digital-adoption-program) says Boost Your Business Technology stopped taking new applications on 19 February 2024.

If someone offers to get you a CDAP grant today, walk away.

## Closed: Alberta Innovates Digital Traction

The [Alberta Digital Traction Program](https://albertainnovates.ca/funding/alberta-digital-traction-program/) provided up to $50,000 to Alberta digital tech companies with software development at the core of their product. It closed on 29 May 2026 and now shows as inactive.

It was never a website grant for a shop or a trade. It was for companies whose product is software. Alberta Innovates replaced it with the [Venture Development Grant](https://albertainnovates.ca/funding/venture-development-grant/), which offers up to $150,000 to Alberta-based, high-growth technology ventures and was open for intake when I checked. If you are building a software product, read that guide. If you run a bakery in Ritchie, it is not for you.

## Open: the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant (training)

The [Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant](https://www.alberta.ca/canada-alberta-productivity-grant), or CAPG, shares the cost of training. The government covers 75 per cent of eligible training costs, up to $5,000 per trainee per fiscal year, with a cap of $100,000 per employer. The employer pays the other 25 per cent.

The parts that matter for a small business:

* **Owners can now be trainees.** Changes effective 8 October 2026 made business owners and employed family members eligible, and set every trainee at 75 per cent. That opens it to owner-operators.
* **Digital skills count.** Eligible training includes "digital and technological skills," along with business operations and technical skills. It has to be instructor-led.
* **It pays for training only.** Tuition, exam fees, textbooks and software for the course. Not wages, not equipment, and not a website build.
* **You need a track record.** The employer must have operated in Alberta for at least a year, be incorporated or registered, be in good standing, and carry WCB coverage for trainees.

One warning. Alberta's general [small business resources page](https://www.alberta.ca/small-business-resources) still showed the older CAPG figures when I checked. Go by the program's own page.

So if you want to learn to run your own Google Business Profile, edit your Shopify store or understand your analytics, a course could be 75 per cent funded. Somebody else building the site for you is not covered.

## Open with a waitlist: BRIDGE, for women entrepreneurs

The [BRIDGE Program](https://www.alberta.ca/bridge-program), run by [Alberta Women Entrepreneurs](https://www.awebusiness.com/bridge), is for women who have been running an Alberta business for one to five years. Participants get training, coaching and planning support, and can access up to $5,000 in financial support.

Cohorts are capped at 25 people. When I checked, the next virtual cohort was set for Winter 2027, with sessions between 11 and 27 January, and no in-person cohort was scheduled. It is a business strategy program, and the money comes with it. It is not a website voucher.

## Open, for Edmonton storefronts: City of Edmonton grants

If your business has a physical shopfront in Edmonton, the City has two programs. Both are on the [business improvement area grants page](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/business_resources/business-improvement-area-grants).

**Storefront Refresh and Signage Grant.** Half of eligible costs up to $1,000 per ground-floor, street-facing unit, for a new or refreshed fascia or projecting sign. Digital signs and billboards are excluded. First come, first served, with six months to finish the work.

**Storefront Improvement Program.** A 50 per cent matching reimbursement up to $50,000, plus up to $10,000 for landscaping, for buildings in a Business Improvement Area or target area. The building has to be at least ten years old and mostly commercial. Design and engineering fees are eligible. The current intake closes on 30 November 2026, and the 2027 intakes are planned for February, April and September.

These pay for your physical frontage, not your website. But a good sign and a clean facade do a lot of the same work, and for a shop on Alberta Avenue or 124 Street they may matter more. My [Edmonton sign permit guide](/blog/edmonton-sign-permits/) has the details on what the grants require.

## Not grants, but useful

* **[Business Link](https://businesslink.ca/)** gives Alberta small businesses one-to-one guidance, webinars and market research at no cost.
* **[Futurpreneur](https://www.futurpreneur.ca/en/)** offers start-up loans of up to $75,000, with mentoring. It is a loan, so it has to be repaid.
* **Tax.** A website is a business expense. How you claim it, and whether any part counts as a capital cost, is a question for your accountant.

## How to spot a grant pitch that is not real

You will see ads promising that "the government will pay for your website." Before you sign anything, ask:

1. **What is the program called?** Get the exact name.
2. **Where is the official page?** It should be on canada.ca, alberta.ca, edmonton.ca or the funder's own site.
3. **Is the intake open today?** Look for an "open" status and a deadline on that page.
4. **Who applies, and who gets paid?** In most programs, you apply and you are reimbursed after you pay.
5. **What happens if the grant is refused?** If you still owe the full invoice, you are buying a website, not getting a grant.
